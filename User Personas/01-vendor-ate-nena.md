# Persona 1 — Ate Nena, High-Volume Vegetable Vendor

> *"Ay oo, gustong-gusto namin. Kasi usually 'yung kukuha dito… para lang maalisan 'yung basura diyan. Pero kung may kapalit, very good."*
> ("Oh yes, we'd really love that. Usually whoever takes it just clears the garbage away. But if there's something in return, very good.")
> — Tita Imelda, vegetable vendor (interview)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stakeholder group** | Baguio public market vendors (about 4,000) |
| **Role in WasteLedger** | Supplier: drops off clean vegetable waste and gets paid per kilo |
| **Priority** | Primary end-beneficiary |
| **Evidence base** | Interviews with Tita Imelda and Tita Mabel, and with Tita Jenny and Tita Bet (vegetable stalls); Concept Note §III, §VI |

## Snapshot
- **Age:** 48 · **Location:** Vegetable section, Baguio City Public Market
- **Business:** Sells assorted highland vegetables, and cleans and trims them **every day** before display
- **Household:** Married with three children; works the stall with a relative or a stall partner
- **Tech:** Android phone; **uses GCash** (every vendor we interviewed has it); Messenger
- **Languages:** Filipino, Ilocano, some English
- **Work hours:** Before dawn until evening, 6–7 days a week

## Background
Ate Nena trims and cleans vegetables every morning. **Interviewed vegetable stalls report 10–15 kg a day, or 70–100 kg a week per stall** (Tita Imelda). Smaller vegetable stalls report about 10 kg, and up to 5 kg on a busy day (Tita Jenny and Tita Bet). She **already segregates** biodegradable waste from residual waste. Sometimes people who keep animals (ducks, rabbits; pigs aren't allowed in the city) take some of her scraps for free. Whatever is left is collected about weekly, usually on Mondays, and **collection is harder when it rains**.

She has been let down before. Her barangay set up a composting facility that *"hindi working ever since"* ("never worked") because nobody managed it, and she thinks of it as money wasted.

## Goals
- **Get something back** for waste she already sorts and gives away for nothing
- **Know where her waste goes.** Her first question was *"Pero ano i-re-recycle niyo?"* ("But what will you recycle it into?")
- **Get paid without effort.** GCash that *"darating na lang"* ("just arrives")
- Keep her stall clean without spending selling time on it

## Pain points and frustrations
- **Laziness at busy times.** *"Minsan po parang nakakatamad… mas madali na lang po siyang ipagsama sa isang plastic"* ("Sometimes it's tiring… it's easier to just put everything in one plastic bag"). Segregation slips when she is rushed.
- **Unreliable collection,** especially in the rain
- **Programs that fizzle out.** The failed barangay composting makes her doubt that a new program will last.
- **Small payout.** At her real volume it is **₱6–9 a day, about ₱156–234 a month** (26 market days at ₱0.60/kg). That is below the concept note's assumption of ₱300 a month at 20 kg/day.

## How she uses WasteLedger
| Step | Touchpoint | What she needs |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Register | Gets a **QR-tagged crate** and a **waterproof supplier ID card**, and links her GCash number | No government ID needed; consent explained in Filipino |
| Drop off | Brings her crate to the station after her morning cleaning | Done in under 1 minute; the **scale display faces her** |
| Feedback | Grade: *clean*, *minor contamination* or *rejected* | The reason for any downgrade, with pictures of what not to include |
| Get paid | GCash payout from the **city's own account** once her balance reaches **₱200** (about every 4–5 weeks at her volume) | Her **balance carries over and never expires**, so a slow week costs her nothing |
| See impact | Monthly SMS: "Your 320 kg became compost at Irisan" | Proof that this program is *working*, unlike the barangay one |

## Design implications
- **No vendor app needed.** Her touchpoints are the crate, the ID card, an **SMS receipt** and the station staff.
  `WasteLedger: 12.4 kg CLEAN. Balance ₱86.20. GCash payout at ₱200.`
- **The crate is provided by the city.** This removes the "one plastic bag is easier" shortcut and matches what vendors asked for.
- **Show where the waste goes.** Put a "where your waste goes" (Irisan → compost) poster at the station and add an impact line to the SMS.
- **Keep the drop-off station open in the rain:** covered and easy to reach.
- **Explain the weekly cap** up front so it doesn't feel like cheating.

## Success measures
- Drops off **at least weekly** (pilot target: at least 70% of vendors active weekly, §IV)
- **Under 5% rejected** loads
- Says the payout arrives on time and is correct

## Open questions
- **Animal-feed collectors:** some of her scraps already go to *ubak* collectors (people who take scraps for animal feed). Should WasteLedger pay only for scraps that would otherwise be garbage, so it doesn't push out a reuse that is better than composting? (This fits the concept note's rule that only newly diverted waste earns the full rate.)
- Is ₱156–234 a month enough to keep her segregating on busy days, or does convenience matter more than money?
