## Snapshot
- **Age:** 52 · **Position:** City Councilor; chairs the Committee on Environment
- **Background:** Former barangay captain; co-sponsored the city's solid-waste ordinances
- **Tech:** Reads briefs on her phone and iPad; her staff prepare documents
- **Pressures:** Public anger over garbage fees, the landfill crisis in the news, the DILG Seal

## Background
Paying suppliers with city money needs a **rate ordinance and a budget line** under RA 9003 §45 and the Local Government Code. She supported **Ordinances 15 and 69, s. 2026** (a 20% waste cut and a ₱75 household fee).

Two pieces of new evidence help her case:
- **Constituents want the government to lead.** *"Dapat sa kanila manggaling 'yung umpisa para sundan ng tao"* ("It should start with them so people will follow," Tita Marlene). They also expect the government to provide the bins.
- **Vendors are willing.** Every interviewed vendor said yes. Most already segregate and give their scraps away for nothing.

## Goals
- A program the city can **defend on cost**: it saves more than it spends
- A **visible environmental win** for the DILG Seal and her constituents
- Show progress on the **Open Government Partnership (OGP) waste-tracking commitment**
- **Not repeat the failed barangay composting project** that vendors still bring up

## Pain points and concerns
- **Optics:** "paying for waste" sounds like a giveaway unless the savings are clear
- **Perverse incentives:** would people make more waste to get paid more? (Weekly cap; the full rate goes only to newly diverted waste.)
- **Unstable costs:** the city's disposal cost dropped from ₱2.24/kg to ₱1.45/kg in 2025. Does the ordinance still hold if it changes again?
- **Budget calendar:** the 2027 budget is already submitted
- **Wasted public money:** she has seen city-funded projects stall without anyone managing them

## How she uses WasteLedger
| Need | What she gets |
|------|---------------|
| Decide | **One-page policy brief:** ₱1.45 vs ₱1.03 per kg, pilot results, projected yearly savings (₱0.5M–₱1.9M) |
| Legislate | **Draft rate ordinance** that ties the payout to the rate-rule formula, so it **adjusts automatically when hauling costs change** |
| Communicate | Public impact numbers and vendor stories (with consent) |
| Oversee | Read-only **quarterly summary** showing the program is managed, not abandoned |

## Design implications
- **Executive summary export** (one page, charts, ₱ saved, tons diverted)
- **Scenario view** at 10%, 20% and 35.9% food-waste shares, and at different hauling costs
- A **public transparency page** she can share
- **Activity indicators** (active stations, payouts made this month) that show the program is alive, which answers the "barangay composting" worry

## Success measures
- Rate ordinance passed and budget line approved after the pilot
- The program is cited in DILG Seal and RA 9003 self-compliance (SCMAR) submissions
- No audit findings on payouts
