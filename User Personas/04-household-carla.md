# Persona 4 — Carla, Baguio Household Resident (General Public)

> *"Maganda talaga kung i-push through ng gobyerno 'yung pag-kolekta ng basura na hiwa-hiwalay. Kasi dapat sa kanila manggaling 'yung umpisa para sundan ng tao."*
> ("It would be really good if the government pushed through with collecting segregated waste. The start should come from them so people will follow.")
> — Tita Marlene (interview), on what the public expects

## Snapshot
- **Age:** 31 · **Location:** A barangay near Session Road, Baguio City
- **Work:** Call-center team lead on the night shift
- **Household:** Lives with her parents and a younger sibling (four people)
- **Tech:** Uses smartphone apps every day: GCash, Shopee, Facebook, Grab
- **Values:** Cares about the environment; follows news about the Urdaneta landfill and the garbage fee increase

## Background
Carla's household produces about **1–2 kg of kitchen scraps a day**. Baguio's Ordinances 15 and 69, s. 2026 set a **₱75 monthly household fee** and a 20% waste reduction target. She segregates because the barangay requires it, but she suspects everything ends up in the same truck. The GSO confirms that residual waste from the barangays goes to the Marcos Highway transfer station and is hauled to Urdaneta **within 24 hours**. That is about **80,472 tons in 2025**.

The vendors we interviewed describe the attitudes she shares: people **expect the government to lead and provide the bins**, and they **won't make a special trip** to redeem rewards. Tita Marlene put it this way: *"nakabihis ka… tapos punta ka SM"* ("you're dressed up… and then you have to go all the way to SM").

## Goals
- **Proof** that her segregation actually matters
- A small reward that **offsets the ₱75 fee**
- **Drop-off close to home** on her own schedule (she works nights)

## Pain points and frustrations
- **Small volumes:** ₱0.60–₱1.20 a day isn't worth a special trip
- **Doubt:** "Is my segregated waste mixed again at the truck?"
- **Inconvenience:** she won't travel to a mall or market to redeem rewards
- **Missed collections:** garbage is *"hindi minsan nakukulekta"* ("sometimes not collected")

## How she uses WasteLedger
| Step | Touchpoint | What she needs |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Register | Barangay drop point or a household QR bin **provided by the barangay** | Quick sign-up; privacy consent she can understand |
| Drop off | Barangay weigh point or on collection day | Close by, flexible hours |
| See impact | Public view: "Your barangay diverted X kg this month" | Visible community progress |
| Reward | GCash, vouchers or a fee credit; **the balance never expires** | Small amounts still add up over time |

## The general public as beneficiaries (even if they never drop off)
- **Lower city costs.** Diverting 1 kg costs about ₱1.03, compared with ₱1.45 to haul and dump it.
- **Less landfill risk.** The Binaliw collapse (36 deaths) and the Urdaneta "imminent danger" listing are public-safety issues.
- **Transparency.** A public dashboard of tons diverted and money saved.

## Design implications
- **Public transparency page** (totals only, no personal data)
- **The barangay provides the bin**, matching what the public expects of government
- Mobile-first self-service suits this tech-comfortable group (unlike vendors)

## Open questions
- Would households drop off for ₱0.60/kg, or do only fee credits or vouchers work at 1–2 kg a day?
- Can barangay collection points handle weighing and grading?
